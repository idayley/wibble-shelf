# The method

The Fundraising kit keeps a pre-seed raise on one canvas and answers one
question every day: **what is the next thing to do, and why?** It does not
raise the money for you. It holds your story, your investor list, the drafts
you will send and your meeting notes, and a strip across the top names the one
thing that is next.

It reads the **Discovery kit** on the same canvas, live. What you learned from
customers is the evidence for your story, so the kit shows Discovery's counts
and fills the hard-question answers from them. It never invents a number that
Discovery doesn't hold.

Three agents help. **Scout** finds and sorts investors, **Asker** drafts the
words, and **Debrief** reads each meeting. You send every message and make
every decision yourself. Nothing here sends, spends or decides.

## Walkthrough

The kit puts four zones under a progress strip.

1. **Story.** The round (amount, cap, dates), what you have learned from
   Discovery, and the five hard questions investors ask. Press **Fill answers**
   and Asker drafts the evidence-based ones.
2. **Investors.** Paste people you know, list who knows whom, or search the
   web, and Scout adds each investor to the board with their kind, stage,
   whether they lead and their check size. Pick one and press **Draft**.
3. **To send.** Asker's drafts: intro requests, notes, replies, follow-ups,
   prep sheets, check-ins and the monthly update. Copy each one, send it
   yourself, press **Sent**.
4. **Meetings.** Drop in notes or a recording. Debrief reads the investor's
   interest, moves them along, and a real next step leaves a **Follow-up
   requested** card in To send, one press from a draft.

Everything the pages show lives in one shared store on your Mac. The agents
write to it and the pages read it. Only the pages work out the phase, the next
step for you and who has gone quiet.

## The phases

The strip shows four phases, from a start date and a close-by date you set in
Story. Prepare lasts about 20 days; first meetings are packed into the
following weeks, with about 15 days before the close; Close follows, then
Backup. If you move the close-by date, the phases move with it and never
overlap.

| Phase | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Prepare | Story, the investor list, first updates to people you know |
| Meetings | First meetings packed close so interest overlaps |
| Close | Leads first, a deadline, SAFEs signed |
| Backup | A second wave or a smaller angel round |

## The nine steps

Each investor moves through Researched, Intro asked, Intro made, First meeting,
Follow-up, Partner meeting, Diligence, Terms, Signed. A pass moves them to
Keep updated, not off the board: a pass now is often a yes next round.

## Reading interest

Debrief gives each meeting one reading, from the investor's own words:

- **Real next step**: an action with a date (a partner meeting, a request for
  references). Moves them a step and requests a follow-up.
- **Maybe**: warm words with no date. Counted as no for now. They stay on the
  monthly update.
- **Pass**: a no. Any bar they named ("come back with revenue") is kept.
- **Intro call**: books a first meeting.

Between two readings, Debrief picks the lower. No quote, no credit.

## Rules the pages and agents follow

- **Nothing is sent, spent or decided for you.** Every draft says it.
- **Short.** An intro request is forwardable as written; a cold note makes one
  ask; a check-in is news, not a nudge; the monthly update has four lines
  (Learned, Did, Next, Ask) and goes to everyone, passes included.
- **Evidence, not adjectives.** Answers and drafts use Discovery's counts and
  quotes only. Quotes appear only when the person consented, and Discovery
  people are never named.
- **Unknown stays unknown.** A check size Scout can't find is "unknown".
- **Quiet is 10 days.** After 10 days without contact, an investor's next step
  becomes "send news".
- **Every zone says what happens there and why**, and every field note names
  its source. Notes paraphrase; none claims to quote a book.

## Sources

Each field note paraphrases one of these. None is quoted.

- Paul Graham, "How to Raise Money" (paulgraham.com): start with people who
  know you, pack first meetings together, treat anything short of a yes as no,
  warm words without a dated action don't count, momentum and the first
  commitment, aftercare for a pass.
- Mark Suster, "Invest in Lines, Not Dots" (Both Sides): investors trust a
  trend they watched more than a single meeting, so send updates before you
  ask; news, not a nudge; a clear pass is useful.
- Brad Feld and Jason Mendelson, *Venture Deals*: leads and followers, what a
  SAFE and its cap are, preparing for a meeting, diligence, reading terms, and
  setting a close date.
- Scott Kupor, *Secrets of Sand Hill Road*: funds need a few huge outcomes, so
  investors listen for how big this can get; the partner meeting.
- The Holloway Guide to Raising Venture Capital: asking for an intro in a form
  that can be forwarded, cold notes as the fallback, replying within a day,
  following up within 24 hours, "why now".
- Jason Calacanis, *Angel*: a smaller angel round as a backup.

One note, the holiday slowdown (most investors go quiet from mid-December to
early January), is widely reported by founders and is not from one book; the
kit says so.

## What the kit cannot do

It cannot tell you whether an investor will back you. It shows who is next,
what you have in hand, and what a dated action looks like, so you spend your
weeks on the investors closest to a yes.
